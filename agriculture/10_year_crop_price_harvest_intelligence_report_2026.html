Why Farm-Gate DECs Trade 15%–30% Below Peliyagoda Manning Market

Sri Lanka’s wholesale produce distribution operates on a hub-and-spoke model anchored by Dambulla DEC (handling ~65% of island-wide vegetable redistribution) and Peliyagoda Manning Market (Western Province terminal hub).

1. Post-Harvest Shrinkage (20%–40%) +12% to +18% Price Markup

Non-refrigerated lorry transport from Keppetipola, Nuwara Eliya, and Thambuttegama in polysacks causes 25%–38% bruising/spoilage on tomatoes, beans, and carrots before reaching Colombo.

2. Diesel & Freight Cost Transmission 2022–2023 Structural Shift

Auto-diesel surged from LKR 104/L (2020) to LKR 430/L (2022), permanently widening the Dambulla-to-Peliyagoda wholesale spread from ~LKR 18/kg pre-crisis to LKR 45–65/kg in 2024–2026.

3. Agrochemical Ban Shock (Apr–Nov 2021) -34% Paddy / -28% Veg Yield

The 2021 chemical fertilizer import ban slashed 2022 paddy output to 3.39M MT (down from 5.15M MT in 2021) and triggered a 2.8x–3.5x surge in wholesale vegetable and rice prices.
